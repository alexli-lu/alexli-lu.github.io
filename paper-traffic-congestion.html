Traffic Congestion and Local Government Fiscal Activities: Evidence from Urban China

Alexis (Li) Lu·Victor Poulin-Therrien (University of Toronto)

Abstract

Based on a panel of Chinese prefectures, this paper examines how traffic congestion shapes local governments’ fiscal behavior. By employing dynamic System GMM estimators and documenting non-linear congestion effects as well as heterogeneity by initial debt burdens, I find that greater traffic congestion is associated with lower debt-to-revenue ratios, with the suppressing effect strongest under mild-to-moderate congestion and weakening at more severe levels.
