Research

Institutions, incentives, and the Chinese economy

I work at the intersection of political economy and applied microeconomics, with China as my empirical setting. My projects ask how formal policy and informal institutions shape the behaviour of governments, firms and communities, and I use large panel data together with careful institutional detail to answer those questions.

Works in Progress

Working paper · Alexis (Li) Lu and Steven Chung (University of Hong Kong)

Clan Culture and Organized Crime: Evidence from China

Abstract Title page

This paper studies the causal effect of clan-based informal institutions on organized crime across Chinese prefecture-level cities. Using proximity to Song Dynasty Neo-Confucian academies as an instrument for clan culture, I find that stronger kinship networks significantly increase organized crime, highlighting the dark side of social capital in weak-governance settings.

Working paper · Alexis (Li) Lu and Victor Poulin-Therrien (University of Toronto)

Traffic Congestion and Local Government Fiscal Activities: Evidence from Urban China

Abstract Title page

Based on a panel of Chinese prefectures, this paper examines how traffic congestion shapes local governments’ fiscal behavior. By employing dynamic System GMM estimators and documenting non-linear congestion effects as well as heterogeneity by initial debt burdens, I find that greater traffic congestion is associated with lower debt-to-revenue ratios, with the suppressing effect strongest under mild-to-moderate congestion and weakening at more severe levels.

Working paper · Alexis (Li) Lu and Zikang Liao (Cardiff University)

The Impact of Trade Tariffs on Corporate Greenwashing: A Strategic Perspective from China

Abstract Title page

This paper examines how tariff exposure shapes corporate greenwashing. I construct a text-based greenwashing measure from MD&A reports using a firm-level panel of Chinese A-share listed companies, and match it with environmental punishment data. I find that lower tariff pressure significantly increases symbolic environmental disclosure.
